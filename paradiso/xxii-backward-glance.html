The Backward Glance

On Saturn's golden ladder, Dante meets Benedict of Nursia, who remembers the founding of his monastery and laments how earthly religious communities have strayed from their beginnings.

Other contemplative lights gather around Benedict. Dante longs to see their true forms, but Benedict tells him this desire must await the highest heaven.

Beatrice signals the ascent. They rise beyond the planetary heavens to the sphere of the Fixed Stars, under Gemini.

Look down, and see how small the world appears.

Dante turns back. The Earth that held his exile, his home, his ambitions, and his grief now appears a tiny patch amid the vast celestial order. The Earth has not shrunk: his perspective has changed.

The alchemical intersection

Saturn taught Dante to avoid inventing what lies beyond his field of view. The backward glance now reverses the difficulty: he sees what was once too large to comprehend from a distance that makes it small.

Nearness can enlarge importance without enlarging truth. Distance can reveal a pattern while concealing details. Neither view is complete by itself.
