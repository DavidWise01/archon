Paradiso XXX · Empyrean · Natural Boundary 030

The River of Light

The nine angelic circles withdraw from Dante's sight. Beatrice leads him beyond the moving heavens into the Empyrean: not a place measured by physical distance or time, but the realm of divine presence in the poem.

Dante first sees a river made of light. Sparks leap from it like living rubies and enter brilliant flowers along its banks, then return to the water.

The same blessed reality is perceived first as flowing radiance, then as an immense white rose.

Beatrice encourages Dante to drink with his eyes from the river, so his sight will be strengthened. As his understanding changes, the apparent river transforms into an enormous circular amphitheater of light: the Celestial Rose, filled with the blessed.

Dante sees the heavenly city open before him. A seat is reserved for Henry VII, whose earthly destiny is not yet complete in the poem's narrated time.
