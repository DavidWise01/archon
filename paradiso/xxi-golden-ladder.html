The Golden Ladder

The Eagle of Jupiter is gone. In the heaven of Saturn, Beatrice withholds the brilliance of her smile, and the blessed do not sing: Dante cannot yet endure their full intensity.

Before him rises a ladder of gold, climbing so far beyond his vision that its end remains unseen. Contemplative souls descend along its rungs.

One light speaks: Peter Damian, who recalls the solitude of prayer and condemns corruption among churchmen.

The ladder extends beyond sight. That is not permission to invent its top.

What can the observer know?

Dante asks why this particular soul has been chosen to greet him. Peter Damian replies that the reason belongs to divine knowledge, beyond even the comprehension of the blessed unless it is disclosed to them.

The alchemical intersection

Mercury asked why we act. Venus asked how we love. The Sun asked what we know. Mars asked what we will endure. Jupiter asked how we judge. Saturn asks whether we can remain still before what we do not know.

In the symbolic kernel, observation is append-only. Each rung becomes evidence of a reached height, not a certificate that the whole ladder has been traversed.
