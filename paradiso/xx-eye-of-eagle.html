The Eye of the Eagle

The eagle speaks again. Within its eye and brow, six luminous souls become visible. Dante discovers that the truth of a life cannot always be determined from its outward record.

David shines at the pupil. Across the brow are Trajan, Hezekiah, Constantine, William II of Sicily, and Ripheus.

The eye sees — but the observer must not presume to see everything.

The apparent exceptions

Trajan and Ripheus seem especially surprising to Dante. The eagle explains their salvation through extraordinary divine grace. It does not invite a human judge to claim access to the unseen judgments of God.

The chromatic boundary

Our symbolic eightfold core and the twenty-four witnesses stay intact, while six selected lights form a focal structure. The six are not new souls created by the selection: they are six named members of the eagle's company.

In our model, attention changes; identity does not. An observation can become more focused without becoming omniscient.

Invariant XX — Focal selection without erasure

Selection is not deletion. The observer concentrates on six lights; the remaining witnesses persist. Neither the selection nor its apparent coherence proves the observer's judgment complete.
