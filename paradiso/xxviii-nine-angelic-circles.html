The Point and Nine Circles

Beatrice turns Dante toward a point of light so intensely radiant that no star could rival it. Around it spin nine circles of angelic beings. The nearest circle burns brightest and turns fastest; the outermost circles are slower.

Dante is puzzled. The order seems reversed from the material heavens, where the outermost celestial sphere is the swiftest. Beatrice explains that the correspondence is not based on physical size, but on the degree of understanding and love.

The innermost are nearest not in miles, but in love.

The nine angelic orders are arranged in three triads: Seraphim, Cherubim and Thrones; Dominions, Virtues and Powers; Principalities, Archangels and Angels. Dante attributes this ordering to Dionysius the Areopagite, while contrasting Gregory's different ordering.
