Canto XXIII · Fixed Stars · Natural Boundary 023

The Triumph of Light

Beatrice watches the heights like a bird awaiting dawn. Then Dante sees light brighter than any he has yet endured: Christ appearing in triumph amid the blessed.

The vision overwhelms him. His sight must be strengthened before he can bear more. A brilliance too great for the observer does not imply a defect in the light.

Capacity is not identity. A threshold is not an ending.

After that glory withdraws, Dante beholds Mary, radiant among the blessed. The angel Gabriel circles her in praise. An immense song of rejoicing fills the heavens as the company honors the Queen of Heaven.

Invariant XXIII — Preserve source and sensor separately

In the experiment, signal intensity and observer capacity are independent controls. An overexposed observer cannot recover missing detail by pretending the light is dimmer than it is. The geometry remains visible at every stage as provenance, while the surrounding glow demonstrates saturation.

In Dante's story, this is a theological and poetic transformation, not an engineering description or a quantum physics experiment.
