## The Measure That Was Not Taken

Along the second terrace, the penitents sat in rough cloaks with their shoulders pressed together. Their eyelids were held closed. They could not follow the sun over the sea or look sideways to see what the next traveler possessed.

But the air carried voices.

“Rejoice for her.”

“Give what you can.”

“May another's good increase.”

Dante listened until he caught himself reaching for the familiar scale: which voice was strongest, whose virtue greatest, whether he himself had ever done as much.

He stopped the comparison before it became a judgment.

“What do I do with these voices,” he asked, “if I do not rank them?”

Virgil answered, “Hear what each gives, without subtracting anything from the next.”

A woman among the penitents turned toward the sound of their footsteps.

“You have eyes,” she said, “and you are learning what we learned too late: another's joy is not a wound made in you.”

Dante did not ask her to prove it. He waited while she spoke.

“In life, I watched the good fortune of my neighbor as though it had been stolen from my own house. Even kindness became an occasion for counting. I knew the price of every blessing and the worth of none.”

“What is different now?” Dante asked.

“I cannot look toward the person beside me to find my place above or below. I must hear her breathe. I must wish her progress without needing it to stop.”

Another penitent began a prayer. Dante did not compare its beauty with the first. The sounds overlapped, and for once the overlapping did not feel like a contest.

Virgil placed a hand on his shoulder.

“You have listened,” he said. “Now we climb.”

At the stair, an angel greeted them. The voice sang of mercy. A second mark grew light upon Dante's brow and was gone. He felt the mountain asking less of his neck, though the lessons had grown no smaller.

Six marks had become five.

They climbed until the generous voices faded.

Ahead, smoke rolled across the path in dense black folds. The sky disappeared. The cliff disappeared. Even Virgil's face became a suggestion in darkness.

Dante reached for the wall and found it cold.

“Is this another punishment?” he asked.

“The third terrace,” Virgil said. “Wrath. Where the eye cannot find an enemy to strike, and the heart must learn another way to answer.”

Somewhere in the smoke, voices sang together. No face could be seen.

A breath of anger rose in Dante at the sudden blindness, the hidden way, the helplessness of not knowing what waited ahead.

He noticed that breath.

He did not give it command.
