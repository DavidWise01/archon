## The Road Without a Map

A crimson cross burned across Mars. Every point of it was a soul, and from the crossing of its beams a voice called Dante by name.

Cacciaguida stood in the light like an ancestor made visible through memory.

“You have come far,” he said, “but the path ahead has a price. There will be loss, exile, and words you will have to speak when silence would be safer.”

Dante looked toward Beatrice.

“Must I know every sorrow before I choose my road?”

“No,” she said. “Even a vision of the future is not the same as living it. Certainty cannot be the price of every worthy act.”

Dante feared what he could not control. He had learned to measure the intentions of deeds in Mercury and to ask what wisdom served in the Sun. Now he saw that a choice might be responsible even when its consequences remained partly hidden.

“Then I accept that I cannot possess the future,” he said.

Cacciaguida did not promise him a painless journey. “Carry what you witnessed. Speak with care. Let the unknown remain unknown until it can be observed.”

Three lights near the cross shimmered separately. One marked what had happened. Another held what had been understood. The third shone toward what might yet happen, without claiming to have seen it.

Dante did not combine them into a false certainty.

The crimson field softened. Beyond Mars, Jupiter opened in a clear and spacious sky. Thousands of stars began to move like living letters; their individual voices would form an eagle of justice without ceasing to be distinct.

Dante followed Beatrice into the next heaven.
