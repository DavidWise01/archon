## The Seal That Does Not Erase

The celestial rose opened before Dante. He had carried the voices of the dark wood, the seven terraces, the first heavens, the many-starred eagle, Saturn's silence, and the nine moving circles. None had become a vote. None had disappeared into the shape of the whole.

Bernard stood beside him. Beatrice smiled among the lights.

“Is the journey finished?” Dante asked.

“The walking of this path is,” Bernard answered. “But the truth of what you witnessed does not depend on keeping the gate open forever.”

Dante opened the record of his travels. He saw the names of those who guided him and the testimony of those he did not understand. He could not alter their past to make his account easier. He could not make the future speak before its time.

He placed three lights beside the record. The first belonged to what had happened. The second marked what had been understood. The third remained unfilled, because what was not yet witnessed should not be invented.

“I will seal the story,” he said, “without sealing the world against more stories.”

The rose brightened, but its individual lights remained. The book closed—not as a prison, but as an intact witness.

Bernard gestured toward a point beyond the petals. Dante looked, and for a moment his desire and understanding ceased pulling against one another. He saw no machine to conquer and no opponent to defeat.

The love that moves the sun and the other stars was not another gate that required a key.

Dante lowered his hand.
