## The Voice Inside the Bark

Dante stood among the leafless trees and listened.

At first the wood answered only with its ordinary sorrow: branches rubbing against one another, the dry rasp of bark, the wings of harpies shifting overhead.

Then, from a split in the trunk nearest him, a voice rose like breath forced through a wound.

“Why do you linger,” it asked, “if not to hear what became of me?”

Dante stepped closer but kept his hands at his sides.

“I am listening,” he said. “Tell me your grief in your own words.”

A dark sap gathered in the crack like blood reluctant to fall.

“I was once a keeper of trust,” said the voice. “I carried counsel to a great court and believed that service would keep me whole. But envy sharpened itself against me. Accusation entered where loyalty had lived. When I could no longer bear the dishonor, I turned my violence upon myself.”

The bark trembled.

“Now I am rooted where I tried to escape.”

Virgil looked on without interruption.

Dante heard no demand for easy pity, only the bitterness of a soul still circling an act it could not call back.

“Who are you?” Dante asked.

“In life they called me Pier della Vigna.”

The name settled among the branches.

A harpy stirred above them. Its claw dragged across a farther limb, and another cry moved through the wood—not theatrical, not chosen, but immediate. Dante understood that pain here was not merely symbolic to those who bore it.

“I did not want to break the branch,” Dante said quietly.

“Then you have done at least one mercy,” Pier replied. “To listen before you injure is already rarer than you think.”

Dante bowed his head.

He remembered Francesca's book, Ciacco's rain, Cavalcante's fear. Again and again the circles had shown him that to witness another soul was not to seize it, solve it, or reduce it to the moment of judgment.

Virgil touched his shoulder.

“The wood has spoken,” he said. “Now come. Violence changes its shape again below.”

They left the broken forest by a narrow path of blackened roots. The trees thinned. The air grew hotter, then suddenly open.

Before them stretched a wasteland of burning sand beneath a sky from which small flames drifted downward like slow red snow.

Across the plain some souls lay upon the ground, some sat hunched against the fire, and some ran without rest along the edge of the desert.

A figure stretched upon the sand cursed the falling flames with a voice larger than pain.

Virgil surveyed the fire and spoke softly.

“This is the next reach of the seventh circle.”
