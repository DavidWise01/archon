## The Silence Between Rungs

The golden ladder rose through Saturn until its last rung disappeared into a darkness filled with stars.

Dante had climbed through thunder, flame, voices, argument, and music. Here he found no song at all.

A contemplative light passed above his head without speaking.

“Shall I climb?” Dante asked.

Beatrice did not answer immediately. She stood beside him and watched the ladder.

Dante felt his old impatience return. To advance seemed better than to remain. To produce an answer seemed wiser than to admit there was no answer yet.

Then he sat at the foot of the ladder.

“I will be still,” he said, “and observe.”

The gold did not stop glowing. The contemplatives continued their ascent. But Dante noticed how different their movements were. One spirit paused between rungs. Another shone steadily while the others passed. A third moved upward only when the next step became clear.

Nothing in their silence required them to become identical.

“Is doing nothing a kind of wisdom?” Dante asked.

“Not always,” Beatrice replied. “There is a difference between patient attention and fear disguised as patience. The difference can be found in what you are willing to see.”

Dante watched longer.

He realized that the ladder was not a machine demanding constant motion. It was a path. The observer could recognize a boundary before crossing it.

“Then the unknown does not order me to fill it with guesses.”

“No,” Beatrice said. “You may preserve a question without inventing its answer.”

Three small lights appeared along the ladder. Dante attended to them separately: what had been witnessed, what he understood now, and what remained reserved for a future observation. None demanded agreement. None pretended to speak on behalf of the others.

At last Beatrice raised her eyes.

The golden ladder faded into a field brighter than the sun. Beneath them, Earth turned like a small, blue point within a vast wheel of stars.

Dante looked down without contempt. The distance had not made human lives less important.

“You have not lost the lesson by becoming still,” Beatrice said. “Carry it into the Fixed Stars. There, faith, hope, and love will ask whether what you profess is what you understand.”

And Dante, still the same traveler, rose.
