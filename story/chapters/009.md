## The Collision That Never Ends

The first stone rolled from the left.

Its carrier strained beneath the weight, heels scraping grooves into the ground. From the right another came, equally burdened, equally certain that his own direction was the correct one.

They met in the middle with a sound like a bell struck inside the earth.

“Why do you hoard?” cried one.

“Why do you waste?” shouted the other.

Then both turned, hauled their stones to the far edges, and began again.

Dante stood beside Virgil and watched.

The people did not stop to hear their own questions answered. They pushed, collided, accused, turned, and pushed once more. The chamber was full of effort, but none of it carried anyone closer to a destination.

“How long have they done this?” Dante asked.

“Longer than either side can measure,” Virgil replied.

“Do they know they are traveling in circles?”

“They know whom to blame for the collision.”

Another crash shook dust from the ceiling.

Dante looked at the grooves in the rock. Every new passage deepened the old tracks. The path was not the reason the stones moved; the endless pushing had made the path.

“Then the other crowd is not what keeps them here,” Dante said.

“No. A burden can become an answer to every question, even when it is the wrong answer.”

Dante watched a man brace his shoulder against the stone. A moment later another man did exactly the same thing, facing the other way. From a distance, the two looked like a reflection split in half.

Virgil led him away before the next collision.

Beyond the circle, the sound of stone striking stone became a murmur, then a hiss. A wet darkness gathered under the archway. The air tasted of iron and stagnant water.

A broad marsh lay ahead.

On its surface, shadows struck and clawed at one another. Beneath the surface, other shadows held their anger so deep that only a low bubbling reached the air.

Dante stopped at the bank.

“Are they still arguing?” he asked.

“Some are,” Virgil said. “The rest have forgotten how to speak.”

The water stirred.
