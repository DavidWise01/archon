## Every Star Still Has a Voice

The eagle formed of Jupiter's countless lights opened its wings above Dante. To an impatient eye, it seemed a single creature, a verdict written in the sky.

Dante looked to Beatrice. “May I hear them separately?”

“Listen,” she replied. “The figure is beautiful, but it does not erase the souls who compose it.”

Dante approached the first star. It told of a judge who had listened after being certain. Its testimony was a memory, not an order.

A second star spoke of a stranger whose goodness had gone unrecognized by the people who should have known. Dante resisted the urge to infer everything from one story.

The third star answered with silence before offering a question: “What do you know of the whole heart from a single act?”

The eagle still held its shape. Yet Dante no longer mistook its outline for complete knowledge of every life within it.

“Must all three agree?” he asked.

“No,” Beatrice said. “Each witness should faithfully report what it sees. Agreement cannot be manufactured, and no quorum can substitute for observation.”

Dante looked back from star to star. Their lights remained distinct; their shared movement was not a forced consensus.

A ladder of gold emerged farther above, climbing beyond sight into the quiet heaven of Saturn.

“Here I learned to listen,” Dante said. “There, I must learn when silence itself carries wisdom.”

Beatrice stepped toward the ladder, and Dante followed.
