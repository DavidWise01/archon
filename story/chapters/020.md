## The Morning Dew

The sea breathed softly against the shore of the mountain.

Dante stood before Cato, still carrying the dust and darkness of the pit he had climbed through. The guardian's face was stern, but not without purpose.

“If you would begin this ascent,” Cato said, “do not bring the soot of Hell upon your brow.”

Virgil bowed his head. He led Dante toward the grass nearest the water, where the first light had not yet burned away the dew.

“Bend down,” Virgil said.

Dante knelt.

Virgil gathered the dew upon his hands and washed Dante's face. The touch was gentle, but it carried an authority different from the judgments below. Black traces of the underworld thinned and ran into the earth.

Dante closed his eyes.

He had crossed many circles by observing what was before him: storms, burdens, false gold, frozen faces, stars. Here the first task was not simply to observe. It was to prepare for change.

“It is a strange mercy,” Dante said, “to be told to cleanse what the journey has marked.”

Virgil answered, “Not every mark must be kept. Some truths are carried inward. Some stains must be washed away before they become mistaken for identity.”

Nearby a slender reed bent with the sea breeze.

Virgil took one and girded Dante with it.

“It yields,” he said, “and yet it rises again. Remember that.”

The mountain lifted before them, no longer an impossible shape at a distance but a path requiring effort. They climbed along its lower slope until the shore shone below like hammered silver.

The ascent narrowed.

At last the stone gathered into a threshold.

Three steps rose before a gate.

The first shone pale as polished marble, reflecting Dante's face with uncomfortable clarity. The second was dark, cracked and rough. The third glowed red as if warmed from within.

Above them stood an angel. Its sword caught the morning light.

Dante felt the washed skin of his face grow cool in the wind.

Virgil stopped beside him.

“This is the gate,” he said. “Every higher passage begins with what you are willing to bring here, and what you are willing to leave behind.”

Dante looked from the white step to the dark, then to the red.

The mountain did not conceal its terms.
