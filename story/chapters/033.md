## The Shape That Love Takes

Venus turned beneath Dante in a music that seemed to have no beginning.

There were lights here that had once loved desperately, imperfectly, and sometimes possessively. Yet the radiance about them did not pretend their earthly stories had never happened. It showed what those stories might become when love no longer demanded ownership.

A luminous spirit approached Dante.

“Must love always remain as it first appears?” he asked.

“If it did,” the spirit replied, “the seed would refuse the tree.”

Dante thought of the name he had spoken before the fire, and of the gaze Beatrice had not allowed him to evade. Desire had once seemed to him a single force: reaching, grasping, holding.

“But if love changes shape,” Dante asked, “how can we know it remains love?”

Beatrice answered. “Ask what it seeks for the beloved. If it wants only possession, it has confused a person with a prize. If it learns to protect another's freedom and flourishing, it may become more faithful by changing.”

The spirit moved through the light, not away from the others but among them.

Dante understood that change need not betray the origin. A memory could be preserved while a habit was relinquished. The past could remain witnessed without becoming a command for the future.

“Then the same heart may learn another form?”

“Yes,” said Beatrice. “Not every form is good merely because it is new. You must still observe what follows from it.”

Dante smiled. Love was no longer a fixed ornament to carry upward. It was a responsibility continually understood.

Above Venus opened the radiance of the Sun. There, two circles of wise spirits would teach him how understanding itself can either divide people into competing camps or make room for truths learned from one another.

He turned toward the brightness.
