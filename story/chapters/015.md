## The Weight of False Gold

Dante watched the bridges of Malebolge hang over one trench after another.

Some were whole. Some were broken. And from a low procession came a glimmer so rich that it seemed a strange mercy to the dark: figures walking beneath gilded cloaks, moving with painfully measured steps.

“Look there,” Dante said. “Why should gold weigh upon the condemned?”

Virgil led him to the edge of the bridge.

The light was false. Each cloak shone on its outer surface, yet where the hem turned Dante glimpsed dull lead within. The figures bent beneath it as though their finery had been forged into a prison.

“Hypocrites,” Virgil said. “What glitters before another's eyes can conceal an unbearable burden from its wearer.”

Dante tried to count the shining figures, but the procession kept advancing. Each person carried a different history, a different reason for wearing a borrowed face. The surface made them look alike.

“Then I should not call them gold,” Dante said.

“Name what you see,” replied Virgil, “and admit what you cannot see. A bright surface is a fact of the light, not proof of the substance beneath.”

Dante found himself remembering the gates of Dis. There too a shape had pretended to be a door. He remembered the stones of the fourth circle: opposing burdens that were easier to accuse than to release. And Pier della Vigna, who had asked only to be heard through the bark.

The difference was not that every shining thing lied. It was that appearance alone could not testify to what lay underneath.

A tremor ran through the bridge. Far below, a false counselor's flame winked like an eye. Ahead, the stone arches began to fail, and a broken path reached toward a rim without any crossing.

Beyond it stood enormous shapes in the darkness.

At first Dante saw towers.

One lifted an arm.

The towers were living beings, half-hidden in a well whose depth swallowed the torchlight. Their shoulders loomed above the abyss; the pit behind them seemed to go on without a floor.

“They are giants,” Virgil said. “The descent ahead cannot be walked.”

Dante looked from the counterfeit gold to those immense bodies. Once more his eyes had found one kind of object where another truly stood.

He placed his hand on the last intact stone and did not step into the dark.
