## What the Water Does Not Take

The river shimmered beneath the branches of Earthly Paradise.

Dante knelt beside its waters but did not immediately touch them.

“Beatrice,” he said, “if the water takes away the sorrow of what I have done, will it also take away what I learned?”

She looked toward the current. “Tell me what you would carry.”

Dante remembered the dark wood. He remembered Virgil, the souls beneath their stones, the voices in the smoke, the hunger of those who could never be satisfied.

“I learned that seeing suffering does not mean I have understood it. That love cannot possess. That being forgiven does not make my actions disappear.”

“Then carry those lessons,” Beatrice answered. “You need not keep the wound open to remember why it mattered.”

Dante put his hand upon his chest.

`{{i}}`

The traveler remained the same traveler. The road behind him was not erased. The burden simply no longer needed to command every step ahead.

Beatrice gestured toward the waters. “The stream releases the hold of guilt, not the truth of what occurred. A lesson kept only as pain is a lesson never allowed to bear fruit.”

They crossed.

For a moment Dante heard the water as though every ripple were another voice telling its own account of the past. None demanded his surrender. None said the past had ceased to exist.

He lifted his gaze. The garden gave way to a brightness no horizon could contain. Beatrice looked upward, and Dante followed her sight toward the first heaven.

The Moon rose before them, silver and uncertain in its light. He would soon meet Piccarda, whose quiet faithfulness would challenge his habit of ranking souls by their apparent position.

“Is the lesson enough?” Dante asked.

“It is enough to bring forward,” said Beatrice. “Now learn what you have not yet seen.”
