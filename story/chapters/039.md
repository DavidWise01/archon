## Nine Circles, Each a Voice

At the threshold of the Primum Mobile, Dante saw a point of light brighter than any sun. Around it nine circles turned, each with its own pace and brightness.

“Shall I look at the center?” he asked.

“In time,” Beatrice replied. “First see the circles without mistaking one for another.”

Dante traced the first path, then the second and third. He continued, one circle at a time, to the ninth. Each deserved observation without being reduced to its neighbor.

“Are the circles competing?”

“A shared center does not require identical paths,” Beatrice answered.

Dante remembered Jupiter's star-built eagle, where each light had its own testimony. Now motion itself carried the lesson: a single diagram cannot replace attention to its parts.

He completed nine observations. None was declared superior. None was compelled to vote. Each could be witnessed as distinct.

“What keeps them in relation?” he asked.

“Love moves the heavens,” Beatrice said, looking into the central radiance. “Their order is not the victory of the loudest circle.”

The nine rings thinned into light. Beyond them a luminous rose opened, seemingly beyond all measure. Beatrice smiled toward her place among the blessed, and Dante prepared for another guide.
