## The Name That Could Not Be Owned

The final wall of fire breathed across the seventh terrace.

Dante could see the penitents moving within it, their voices raised in songs of love purified of conquest. The flames did not resemble the fires he had known below. They asked him to pass onward rather than hold him captive.

Yet the heat frightened him.

Virgil stood by his side. “You cannot reach the garden by standing here forever.”

Dante looked into the fire and remembered one beloved name: **Beatrice**.

He spoke it aloud.

It was not a summons. He did not expect the woman whose name he loved to appear and reward his courage. He spoke as one who admits that love has meaning beyond the wishes of the one who loves.

“Beatrice,” Dante said again, more quietly.

Virgil turned toward him.

“Do you speak her name to keep her?” he asked.

“No,” Dante answered. “I think I have tried to keep too many things. I speak it because she is not mine to hold, and because I must still answer for what I have done with the love I was given.”

Virgil nodded. “Then let the name lead you forward, rather than back into possession.”

He stepped into the flame. Dante followed.

Heat surrounded them on every side, yet it did not devour their purpose. Through the rushing air he heard the other penitents singing. Their voices did not erase desire; they gave it a different direction.

Dante held to his guide's voice through the passage.

At the far side, the last mark lifted from his brow.

No mark remained.

The mountain path rose one final time beneath the stars. They rested until morning, and when the first light appeared they climbed the remaining steps.

Trees stood above the cloud line. A clear stream crossed a gentle meadow. Birdsong sounded where no sinner cried for judgment.

The air was green with beginning.

“This is the Earthly Paradise,” Virgil said.

Dante turned toward him, ready to ask what came next. But there was a stillness in the guide's expression that had not been there before.

“I have guided you with what reason and love of truth could give,” Virgil said. “Beyond this garden, another voice must lead.”

Dante heard movement across the grass.

A procession came through the trees in brightness and song. At its heart stood Beatrice, not as a prize waiting at the end of his journey, but as a person whose clear gaze demanded he face his wandering.

The name he had spoken in the fire now stood before him.

Dante did not reach for her.

He stood still.

“Look at me,” Beatrice said.

The garden seemed to hold its breath.
