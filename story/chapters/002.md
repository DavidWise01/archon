## The Hunger That Has No End

Dante did not run.

The leopard crossed above him in a flash of spotted gold. On the upper ridge, the lion breathed into the morning, and sunlight caught the edges of its mane.

But Dante watched the she-wolf.

She was all ribs and restless shadow, a creature made thin by wanting. At first, he thought she was hungry because she had too little. Then she sniffed at the mountain, the trees, the sky—at everything she had not yet taken—and he understood that she would remain hungry even if she swallowed the world.

Her paws made no sound on the stone. Wherever she looked, the path seemed to narrow.

Dante reached for the small light that had brought him here, but it was gone.

The wolf stepped toward him.

A voice rose from below the slope.

“Not that way.”

A figure stood among the pale rocks: an older traveler with a poet's patient gaze. His cloak stirred in a wind Dante could not feel.

“You have seen the hunger,” the stranger said. “Now you must learn the road that does not feed it.”

Dante looked back at the mountain. The summit still shone. It had not vanished; it had merely refused the shortest path.

“Who are you?” he asked.

The stranger answered, “I was a poet. Call me Virgil.”

Behind them, the she-wolf watched.

Ahead of them, another road opened into the dark.
