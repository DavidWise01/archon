## The Anger Underwater

Dante did not watch the fighters on the surface.

He knelt at the bank of the Styx and listened beneath.

At first there was only the slap of black water, the splashing of bodies, and the ugly struggle of the openly wrathful. Then he heard a trapped muttering, low and broken, as though speech were trying to remember itself through mud.

“What are they saying?” he asked.

Virgil stood beside him.

“They say what anger says when it is swallowed instead of spoken. Not every wrath strikes outward. Some bury it until it can no longer become words.”

Bubbles rose in slow, unpleasant breaths.

Dante could not make out a whole sentence. He heard only fragments: a protest never finished, an injury rehearsed too long, a bitterness mistaken for shelter. The voices were not silent. They were smothered.

“So even silence can be furious,” Dante said.

“Open rage lashes the world,” Virgil answered. “Buried rage drowns within itself. Both remain bound to what they cannot release.”

A boat struck the bank. Its ferryman, Phlegyas, shouted at the travelers. Virgil answered with quiet authority. Dante stepped aboard and watched the marsh close behind them.

They crossed between shades striking one another at the surface and others whose sorrow and anger stirred the mud from below.

At the far shore, iron-red towers rose above the water. Their glow seemed to come from deep within the metal itself. Between the marsh and the city stood a gate that had already decided to refuse them.

Virgil stepped onto the black stone.

“That is the City of Dis.”

The door stayed shut. Above it, red light moved like embers behind a furnace wall.

“Will they open it?” Dante whispered.

“We must arrive before we can learn what bars the way.”

Behind them the Styx hissed against the shore. Before them stood the iron city.
