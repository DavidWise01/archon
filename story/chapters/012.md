## The Names That Outlive the Fire

Farinata rose from the burning tomb as if the flames were merely the light of a council chamber.

Dante remembered Ciacco's rain-soaked warning about Florence. He looked not at the fire but at Farinata's face.

“Tell me about the living,” Dante said.

“Which living?” Farinata answered. “Those who still rule Florence, or those whose names they have tried to erase?”

“Both.”

Farinata spoke of banners and exiles, of his own house, the Uberti, and of the rivalry that had followed him even into death. He asked after the families of Florence as though a city's soul could be read from a list of its enemies.

Another shade lifted himself from a nearby tomb. He was Cavalcante, and his question came not from pride but fear.

“My son Guido,” he asked. “Does he still walk beneath the sun?”

Dante hesitated, confused by the tense in which the dead spoke of distant events. At that hesitation the father heard a catastrophe that Dante had not said. Cavalcante sank from sight.

“Wait!” Dante cried.

He had meant no harm. Yet silence, too, could be mistaken for an answer.

Farinata stood straight as before, but Dante heard the distance in his certainty. The dead could speak of tomorrow while being unsure what was happening among the living today.

“So you know some things far away,” Dante said, “but not the face nearest the door?”

Farinata did not deny it.

Dante thought of every name he had carried through the circles: Francesca, Paolo, Ciacco, Virgil. They were not labels for judgments. They belonged to people whose stories continued beyond the little part he had witnessed.

“Remember them without taking possession of them,” Virgil said.

The poets left the burning avenues. A rough path wound down between scorched stones until the air ceased to shimmer. Ahead, a forest rose under a bruised sky.

Its trees had no leaves. Their branches were knotted like fingers caught in a final plea. Harpies watched from the upper limbs.

A wind passed through the wood.

Somewhere inside the bark, a human voice trembled.

Virgil stopped. “This is the broken wood of the seventh circle. Attend to what has happened here.”

Dante looked at the nearest branch.

He did not reach to break it.
