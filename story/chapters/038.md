## The World That Remained Worth Loving

From the heaven of the Fixed Stars, Dante looked back at Earth. It was small and blue, surrounded by a darkness lit with distant fires.

He had once thought elevation meant leaving the lesser world beneath him. But he recognized the rivers, the roads, the people whose lives could not be reduced to the mistakes he had witnessed.

“Do they look smaller to you?” Beatrice asked.

“Only in distance,” Dante replied. “Not in worth.”

He remembered the dark wood where his own feet had strayed. He remembered the souls who carried stones, the voices in the smoke, and the unnamed good deeds that no earthly crown would reward.

“If I judge them from so far above, I might forget that I was among them.”

“And that you are still answerable to them,” Beatrice said.

Dante watched the turning globe. Across it, joy and sorrow continued without waiting for his understanding. He did not invent what he could not see. He did not pretend the globe's small outline was a complete account of those who lived upon it.

“I look back with mercy,” he said, “because distance has not given me their whole stories.”

A bright point appeared ahead. Around it nine circles turned, not as chains but as a moving order whose meaning was love.

Beatrice guided him onward. The world behind them was not discarded; its people remained part of what Dante must carry forward.
