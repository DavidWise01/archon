## A Door That Is Not There

Dis was made of doors that would not open.

Iron-red towers rose from the black bank, and the great gate seemed to have learned the art of refusing before Dante or Virgil had spoken. High above, the defenders called down a command: turn back.

Virgil stepped forward to answer. Dante remained by the wall, watching a seam between two blocks of stone.

“Perhaps there is another door,” he said.

He followed the fortress along its outer edge. The masonry was warm against his palm. Emberlight crawled through narrow cracks. At each bend he expected a postern, a stair, some hinge the guards had forgotten.

There was none.

He came upon an arch half-buried in rock and found that it had been filled entirely with iron. Another apparent opening proved to be a shadow. He found a keyhole without a key, then discovered it had been carved only to resemble one.

The walls offered the shape of possibility, but never its substance.

Virgil caught up with him.

“You were right to look,” the guide said. “But finding no door does not mean you failed to see.”

“Then what does it mean?”

“That this obstacle is real. Do not spend your whole journey inventing openings where there are none.”

A distant cry broke across the marsh. The gate shuddered, and a figure came over the black water without boat or bridge. The messenger touched the iron as casually as one might lift a curtain.

The defenders scattered. The gate opened.

Dante stared.

“I searched everywhere,” he said.

“And now the way has changed,” Virgil replied. “Notice who opened it. Do not pretend your search alone moved the hinges.”

They passed through Dis.

Within its walls, the ground was divided into avenues of burning sepulchers. Red fire glowed along their open lids. From one tomb rose a voice proud enough to challenge the city itself.

A tall figure stood amid the flames, unbowed.

“Who walks through my country?” it asked.

“Farinata,” Virgil said quietly.

Dante looked over the fields of tombs. Here certainty had been given walls of its own.
